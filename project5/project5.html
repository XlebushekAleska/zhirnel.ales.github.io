Overview

From entropy to communication

Entropy describes uncertainty in a source. Mutual information measures how much observing one variable tells us about another, while channel capacity describes the greatest reliable information rate supported by a communication channel.

Four related quantities

For two random variables X and Y, joint entropy describes their uncertainty together. Conditional entropy describes the uncertainty that remains in one variable after the other is known.

Joint entropy H(X,Y)
Conditional entropy H(X|Y)
Mutual information I(X;Y)
Channel capacity C = max I(X;Y)

Why this completes the chain

Earlier projects studied probability, entropy, compression and error-correcting codes. A noisy channel connects these ideas: uncertainty enters during transmission, mutual information tells us how much of the input survives, and coding can be used to communicate reliably below the channel capacity.

Equivalent definitions of mutual information

Mutual information is the reduction in uncertainty about X after Y is observed. The same quantity can also be written using joint entropy or by reversing the roles of X and Y.

Core identities I(X;Y) = H(X) − H(X|Y) I(X;Y) = H(Y) − H(Y|X) I(X;Y) = H(X) + H(Y) − H(X,Y)

How to read the result

If X and Y are independent, knowing Y does not reduce uncertainty about X, so their mutual information is zero. If Y determines X perfectly, the mutual information can reach the entropy of X.

Independent example

Four cells equal to 0.25

X and Y are both fair binary variables, but they are unrelated. H(X) = H(Y) = 1 bit while I(X;Y) = 0 bits.

Perfect dependence

P(0,0) = P(1,1) = 0.5

Y always equals X. One variable completely reveals the other, so I(X;Y) = 1 bit.

Part 02

Binary Symmetric Channel

A Binary Symmetric Channel (BSC) transmits a bit correctly with probability 1 − p and flips it with probability p. For the usual noise range 0 ≤ p ≤ 0.5, capacity falls from 1 bit/use to 0.

Empirical mutual information

The simulator also estimates a 2×2 joint distribution from the transmitted and received bit pairs and calculates mutual information from those observed frequencies.

Important distinction

Capacity is not an uncoded success rate

Channel capacity is a theoretical reliable communication rate when sufficiently powerful coding is allowed. It is not the percentage of bits that survive one uncoded transmission.
